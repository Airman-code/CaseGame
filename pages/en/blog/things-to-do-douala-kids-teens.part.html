Things to do in Douala with children or teenagers

By the Case Game team · 30 October 2026

In short: in Douala, young people can keep busy in video game venues, leisure centres in shopping malls, outdoor activities like Watersoft, and workshops run by associations. The right choice depends on age, budget and the time available. Here are our ideas, with prices where we know them.

1. A supervised video game venue

Video game venues remain the favourite outing for 10 to 18-year-olds. For parents, the key criterion is supervision: staff on site, age-appropriate games (PEGI ratings) and clear opening hours.

At Case Game, in New Bell (Hôtel Fatime Babylone), half an hour of PS4 or Xbox costs 350 FCFA, an hour 600 FCFA, and the Wii 350 FCFA an hour. The After School pack (500 FCFA, 4 pm to 6 pm) is designed for after class. The venue is open Tuesday to Sunday; children under 10 come with an adult.

2. A game of Watersoft with friends

To get moving, Watersoft is a team tactical shooting game with water beads, far less painful than paintball. At Case Game, it is open from age 8, supervised by our staff, with protective gear provided, for 300 FCFA a game. It is also a great idea for a group birthday.

3. A tournament to test yourself

Competitive teenagers love tournaments: they learn to handle pressure and to respect the rules and their opponents. Case Game runs a tournament every week (200 FCFA entry) and a free Wii League. The monthly tournament with a cash prize is for players aged 18 and over.

4. A family leisure centre

For a family day out with younger children, shopping malls have play areas. Douala Grand Mall, opened in late 2020, is home for example to the Loisiland family leisure centre and children's play areas (Africanews, Maligah). Check opening hours and prices before you go.

5. Free workshops to learn differently

Several associations in Douala offer educational activities. The Association Case Game runs free workshops on African history, geography, art and citizenship, as well as the Gamer Solidaire et Vert programme: useful missions for the neighbourhood (clean-ups, recycling, helping residents) rewarded with play time.

6. Combine games and school success

During the school year, the best compromise is often to link leisure with homework. Case Game tutoring (primary school to final year, from 5,000 FCFA a month) rewards effort with Games Points that can be exchanged for play time.

Our tips for parents

  • Check the supervision: staff on site and rules on display.
  • Look at the games on offer: they should suit your child's age.
  • Set a budget and a time limit: packs and passes often work out cheaper than single sessions.
  • Choose places you can reach by phone or WhatsApp, so you know where your child is.
